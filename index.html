Centaurus A: Point-Source Census Across the Giant Radio Lobes

Ongoing

Chandra · source detection · completeness · logN–logS

A reproducible rebuild of the X-ray point-source census across Chandra ACIS-I pointings along the giant radio lobes of the nearest radio galaxy — detection, photometry, hardness ratios and spectral diagnostics, with completeness-corrected source counts, scripted end to end. The question the analysis is built to answer: once the survey selection function and the cosmic X-ray background are accounted for, is there a compact-source population physically associated with the lobes, and how sensitive is the inverse-Compton magnetic-field estimate to point-source contamination? A draft is with co-authors.

  • CIAO
  • wavdetect
  • srcflux
  • XSPEC
  • Python

Relativistic Reflection, Black-Hole Spin & Jet–Accretion Coupling

In preparation

XMM-Newton · NuSTAR · XSPEC · relxill

Joint XMM-Newton and NuSTAR spectral modelling with relativistic reflection (relxill) and MCMC to constrain black-hole spin across a multi-target sample — built around a documented software stack, explicit treatment of degeneracies between spin, inclination and coronal parameters, and a fitting pipeline that can be re-run end to end.

  • XSPEC
  • relxill
  • NuSTAR
  • XMM-Newton
  • MCMC

X-ray Signatures of Radio-Mode AGN Feedback

Proposal

galaxy groups · poor clusters · hot gas · Chandra · XMM-Newton

A proposed archival programme on how the balance between AGN mechanical output and radiative cooling depends on the dynamical state of the host atmosphere, in the group regime where the shallow potential makes feedback most consequential — measuring the AGN-driven perturbation of the hot gas wherever it can be measured, rather than only in the minority of systems where a cavity is cleanly detectable.

  • Chandra
  • XMM-Newton
  • APEC
  • hierarchical models

RAD@home CST Radio Galaxies — X-ray Environments

Collaboration

Chandra · intracluster medium · radio/X-ray overlays

As a remote X-ray collaborator with the RAD@home Astronomy Collaboratory (PI Prof. Ananda Hota), I analyse the cluster and group environments of Collimated Synchrotron Threads (CSTs), a newly recognised class of filamentary structure in radio galaxies. Lead case: CST-2 in Abell 2092, where archival Chandra data show a ≈2.7 keV intracluster atmosphere whose thermal pressure far exceeds that of the radio thread — evidence that the surrounding hot gas confines and shapes the structure.

About

Biography

I am an X-ray astrophysicist. My research asks how matter accretes onto supermassive black holes, how that accretion couples to relativistic jets, and what the X-ray emission of active galaxies can tell us about both. I completed my doctorate at the Astronomical Observatory of the Jagiellonian University in Kraków on fluorescent iron lines in radio-loud AGN, and continued as a postdoctoral fellow at National Tsing Hua University in Taiwan.

The methods I work with run from broadband spectral modelling of a single nucleus to source detection and completeness correction across wide fields: relativistic reflection and Fe K diagnostics, the separation of a weak nucleus from surrounding jet- and ISM-related emission, low-count statistics, and the X-ray view of jets, hotspots and lobes. I work across Chandra, XMM-Newton and NuSTAR, and I care that an analysis can be re-run from raw data to figure by someone who is not me.

My first degree was in mechanical engineering, and I have since worked in instrumentation, data engineering and quality engineering between research posts. That background is a supporting strength rather than a second career: it is where the systems thinking, the pipeline discipline and the comfort with large, imperfect datasets come from. The research question has been the same one since 2014.

I work from Bengaluru, India, and I am looking for a postdoctoral position or research fellowship in X-ray astronomy and AGN physics.

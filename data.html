The computation behind the astrophysics

Every result on this site rests on code: multi-terabyte X-ray datasets reduced with scripted pipelines, spectral models fitted under proper low-count statistics, and analyses written so they can be re-run from raw data. This page collects that computational side, together with the applied data- and quality-engineering roles held between research posts, and two granted patent families in optical light-guide design.

Capabilities

What the computational work involves

Statistical inference

Low-count Poisson regimes, Bayesian modelling and MCMC, upper limits, and honest treatment of degeneracy and selection.

Python pipelines

Scripted, version-controlled reductions replacing manual steps — from raw observatory events to final figures.

Provenance & documentation

Recorded software versions, calibration files and parameters, so a result can be reproduced years later.

Large scientific datasets

Multi-terabyte multi-mission archives; batch processing on Linux and cluster environments.

Simulation-based validation

Injection-and-recovery tests that measure a pipeline's own detection and measurement bias instead of assuming it away.

Instrumentation & engineering

Optomechanical design and QC/test experience — systems thinking carried over from a mechanical-engineering first degree.

Patents

AI-optimised solar light-guide systems

Co-inventor on granted patents applying AI and optimisation to optical light-guide systems for solar energy capture, combining optical physics with data-driven design. The patent numbers are the authoritative reference:

  • US 11,668,926 B2
  • TW 109125878
  • TW 110124889

Patent titles vary across source documents, so the numbers are given rather than a single title. This work is engineering, not astrophysics, and is listed here rather than under research.
